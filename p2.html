Where the Dreamlight Burns

by Rish

I dreamt I swallowed a silver sun,
it melted down my throat like wine,
the stars applauded, one by one,
and the moon said, “Child, you’re mine.”

I built a kingdom in my mind,
its walls were stitched from threads of time,
each river sang, each tree aligned—
but all dissolved by morning’s chime.

For when I woke, the sky was ash,
the mirror showed a stranger’s face,
the golden fields reduced to trash,
the galaxies fell out of place.

The clock ticks on in bitter jest,
a metronome for broken hearts,
but still, I cradle dreams to rest,
though daylight tears them all apart.

For dreams, you see, they softly burn,
a hunger I can’t seem to quell,
and though reality will spurn,
I’ll chase the dreamlight into hell.
