The Rope Still Hangs

by Rish

I still see it.
The rope.
The beam.
The note you left unfinished.

You wrote "I tried,"
and that was enough to end you.

I should have known.
I should have seen it in the way your hands trembled
when you said you were fine.
I should have counted the pills you didn't take,
the dinners you didn’t eat,
the phone calls you didn’t return.

But I believed you.
God, I believed you.

Now the rope lives here too —
coiled in the corners of every room,
snaking across ceilings and doors.

I read the last text you sent,
every night,
looking for a word I missed.
A clue.
A scream in disguise.

If I had stayed five minutes longer,
would it have changed anything?

They tell me time heals.
They don't know how memory works —
how it strangles softer than any rope,
how it tightens each year instead of loosening.

You forgave me in death.
But I can’t forgive myself in life.

The rope still hangs.
Not around my neck,
but somewhere worse —
around my heart.
