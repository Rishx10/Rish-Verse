The Rouge In My Throat

“They were never meant for daylight.”

When moonlight drapes its velvet veil,
I wander alleys stitched with shadows,
the scent of smoldering roses on my breath.
I search for you—
my nightfall, my flickering flame.

Your eyes—embers beneath a cathedral sky,
your lips—embers that scorch my wrist.
Nocturnal lullabies tangle like silk,
their verses echoing in the dark.
To bite, or not to bite…

My fangs tremble in their sheath.
Streetlamps glow soft in my longing,
each flicker a ghost of your name.
Your voice—a shiver in the dark,
a blade slipping beneath my ribs,
an oath pressed in wax and wine.

Come to me, my crimson fire—
I will sip the heat from your pulse,
write sonnets in the rouge of your throat,
and leave my mark in the hollow of your sigh.

Let the night bear witness.
Let the alley remember.
Let the moon whisper:
“They were never meant for daylight.”
